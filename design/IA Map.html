01
Drafts deserve a home.
Your half-finished tournament can't live in a sheet — it gets lost the second you tap somewhere else. The Create page persists in-progress work at the top of the screen so resuming is one tap, not a memory test.
02
Templates need somewhere to live.
Most organizers run the same tournament every season. A real tab gives saved formats, default rosters, and reusable schedules a permanent shelf. A modal sheet has no shelf.
03
Three creation primitives ≠ three buttons.
Match, Team and Tournament aren't equal: a match takes 2 minutes, a tournament takes a week. Cards beat buttons — they carry weight, time, audience, and badges (Most common · For captains · For organizers) that help users pick correctly the first time.
04
A page can grow. A sheet can't.
Future additions — auto-tournament from a fixture list, AI scorecard import, "create from this match" remix — all need real estate. A real page absorbs them; a sheet would balloon into a menu.

Discover → top-bar search icon (⌖), reachable from every tab
Discover isn't a destination people visit daily — it's a tool they use when they need to find a team, a tournament, a player, or a venue. That's exactly what a top-bar search icon is for. Tap ⌖ from any tab → a full-height Discover sheet slides up with: Browse near you · Tournaments by format · Teams looking for opponents · Players free-to-pick · Trending. Same screen, reachable in one tap from anywhere.
Pavilion = your command center, anchored in the middle
The center slot of the bottom bar is the most-tapped real estate on a phone — your thumb naturally rests there. It belongs to your most-used personal action, which in this app is managing your stuff and starting new things. Pavilion holds your teams · your tournaments · your fixtures · your drafts · saved templates · stats you've recorded · invites you've sent, with the three creation cards (Match · Team · Tournament) pinned at the top. Profile is separate — it lives under the avatar.
Why “Pavilion”? In cricket it's the players' building beside the ground — dressing room, kit, scorebook, captain's office. The exact metaphor for a personal workspace tab in a cricket app. One word. No verb. Strong DNA.
PAVILION → PICK Dugout Dressing Room Squad Hub Mine HQ
Avatar (top-right) = your public profile
Your avatar opens your player profile — the same page anyone else sees when they tap your name (career, form, wagon-wheel, posts). Edit and settings hang off a “⋯” on that profile page. This keeps a clean split: Pavilion is your command center, Avatar is your face to the world.
Messages = DMs only. Inbox = system notifications.
These are different beasts and shouldn't merge. Messages tab = captain-to-captain, player-to-player threaded conversations (date negotiation, banter, recruitment chat). Inbox bell (top-bar) = system events: match requests, tournament invites, score disputes, milestones. Messages have a human on the other side; Inbox items have the system on the other side.

Discover — four ways to fit it in. The verdict.

Where Discover lives
Trade-off
Verdict
As a 6th bottom-nav tab
Highest visibility, but kills tap targets (≤56px each on iPhone) and feels like a menu. Apple HIG and most native apps cap at 5.
✕ NO
As a top-bar icon (⌖) on every screen
Always one tap away. Doesn't compete with daily-use tabs. Free real estate. Same pattern as Twitter, Threads, Instagram.
✓ YES
As a sub-tab inside Feed
Hidden. Users won't think to look there — Feed is a verb ("read what's new"), Discover is a verb ("find something new").
✕ NO
As a section inside Tournaments + Matches tabs
Good supplement — a "Browse" rail at the top of those tabs surfaces nearby teams/tournaments contextually. Use this in addition to the top-bar search icon.
✓ also
As an FAB (floating button) on Feed
Conflicts with iOS's bottom-bar paradigm. FABs feel Android-y. Skip.
✕ NO

Final structure: 5 bottom tabs in order — Feed · Tournaments · Pavilion (center) · Matches · Messages — plus 3 persistent top-bar surfaces (Discover ⌖ · Inbox ◔ · Avatar = profile). Pavilion sits in the thumb-friendly middle slot because it's where you'll spend the most time managing your teams, tournaments and drafts.

08 · Pavilion · the deep dive
most-tapped tab

The command center, in full.

Pavilion is the third tab — the one your thumb rests on. It's where you go to do things on this app: resume a half-finished tournament, see today's fixture, RSVP to a match invite, check your stats, browse what you saved, manage your team or tournament. It's also the only tab whose shape changes with who you are: a casual player sees a calm, tight Pavilion; a captain sees a roster-management panel surface; a tournament organizer sees registrations and schedule duties. Same address, three faces.

PRINCIPLE 01
Status before identity, action before browsing.
Pavilion opens with a "what needs you" strip — pending invites, drafts to resume, a fixture starting in 90 minutes — not a vanity card. Action surfaces (Match · Team · Tournament creation) sit second. Lists of your stuff sit third. Identity ornaments (avatar, badges) live in the top bar, not here.
PRINCIPLE 02
Role-aware, not role-switched.
One Pavilion. No "switch to captain mode" toggle. Your captaincy of Bandra Royals and your organizer-ship of MPL '25 are contextual surfaces that grow inline when you have duties to do, and quietly disappear when you don't. Captains see a captain block; organizers see an organizer block. Most users see neither.
PRINCIPLE 03
Yours, not theirs. Pavilion ≠ Profile.
Pavilion is your workspace — drafts, fixtures, saved items, calendar. Your profile (the public-facing player page anyone can view) lives behind the avatar in the top bar. Pavilion never shows your profile preview, nor edits to it. The split keeps each surface focused.

The full architecture, by zone.

Pavilion has six zones. They scroll vertically on Pavilion Home; tapping any header jumps to a dedicated section page. Role pills mark which roles see each leaf — ALL = every user, CAP = surfaces only when you captain ≥1 team, ORG = surfaces only when you organize ≥1 tournament.

Pavilion is a single tab that quietly does the work of three.

We didn't add a captain tab, an organizer tab, or a "manage" tab. We let one tab read the situation and surface the right block. The result: 16 distinct screens, 5 home-state variants, three role-aware overlays — all reachable from one slot in the bottom bar.

01 Six zones, one scroll — Status, Create, Calendar, Your stuff, Library, Settings. Order is fixed; visibility is contextual.
02 Five home faces — New user · Player · Captain · Organizer · Power user. Same scaffold, different status block.
03 Sixteen drill-down screens — every leaf has a dedicated page with its own empty state, full state, and filters.
04 No mode switch — captaincy and organizership are roles, not modes. They surface inline when relevant and vanish when not.
09 · Spec coverage map

Every feature in the spec, mapped to a surface.

The eight features of the spec — Identity, Teams, Tournaments, Match & Scorecard, Stats & Rankings, Social, Discovery, and the synthesis layer — broken into their atomic capabilities and pinned to the IA surface that owns them. Coverage verdict: every capability called out in the 8-feature spec lands on a screen, an inbox row, a manage tab, a settings toggle, or a deferred placeholder. MVP is heavy at the bottom (live scoring, claim flow, fixture generation — that's where the engineering risk concentrates). Upper-funnel social and discovery are mostly off-the-shelf shapes. Pavilion swallows what was previously scattered across "+ Create", "Profile", and "Settings" — three buckets folded into six zones, role-aware.
